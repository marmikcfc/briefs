Marc Andreessen: "Software is about to eat the world much faster" - a 15-year essay update that is also a $48B funding announcement

September 10, 2026, ~10:00 AM IST · X Article posted 12:33 AM ET (~9.5h old): 404K views, 3.2K likes, 419 reposts, 130 replies · the news underneath: Cognition's $2B+ Series E at a $48B valuation (Sep 8), co-led by a16z · no HN thread on the essay or the round found as of scan time · blow-up: medium-high (founder's-tribune thesis + contested vendor metrics + a skeptic reply layer already mocking the "1000x engineer")
The gistFifteen years after "Why Software Is Eating the World," Marc Andreessen has posted the sequel: tech went from 31.5% to 94.4% of the market cap of the world's ten biggest companies while only 1 in 300 humans wrote software, every line by hand - "software has been eating the world at the speed of human hands. It is about to eat the world at the speed of compute." His evidence: Devin, Cognition's coding agent, went from writing 13% of Cognition's own production code to more than 90% in a year, so the 10x engineer becomes the 100x engineer, "the 1000x engineer isn't too far behind," and job doom is wrong because human wants are infinite (he cites Milton Friedman). The essay is simultaneously the public face of a16z co-leading Cognition's $2 billion raise at a $48 billion valuation two days earlier - every load-bearing number in it is a company or customer case-study metric, and the essay ends "I know where I'm putting my money." The replies noticed.

What happened

Andreessen posted the piece as an X Article at 12:33 AM ET on Sep 10; the identical text ran on a16z.com as "Investing in Cognition" on Sep 9. The occasion: Cognition announced a $2B+ Series E at $48B on Sep 8 (Reuters), nearly double its $26B mark from May, led by new investors a16z and Accel with Founders Fund, General Catalyst, Avenir and 30+ others including Nvidia. The essay's concrete claims: Devin writes 90%+ of Cognition's production code (up from 13% a year ago); Mercedes-Benz turned an 8-month COBOL migration into 8 days; Rivian 10x'd test generation; Itau auto-remediates 70% of security vulnerabilities with Devin. Scott Wu quote-posted: "a16z backed my first company. Really excited for the chance to run it back."

How we got here

  1. Aug 20, 2011: the original essay. "Why Software Is Eating the World" in the WSJ argues every company is becoming a software company. It aged well enough that a sequel writes itself.
  2. March 2024: Devin launches. Cognition introduces "the first AI software engineer" with SWE-bench claims; early demos draw cherry-picking accusations. The company keeps shipping anyway and lands enterprise logos (Mercedes-Benz, Goldman Sachs, Citi, NASA).
  3. 2024-25: the percentage race. Big tech CEOs compete to say what share of their code is AI-written (Google, Microsoft, YC batches). The metric is never defined the same way twice.
  4. May 2026: Series D. Cognition raises $1B+ at $26B (Lux, General Catalyst, 8VC); disclosed run-rate revenue: $492M.
  5. Summer 2026: a16z exits Cursor. TechCrunch notes a16z was a major Cursor backer and "made a killing when it sold to SpaceX" - so this round is a16z re-entering AI coding by backing Cursor's direct rival. Forkast reports Cognition rejected a SpaceX acquisition offer before this round.
  6. Sep 8-10, 2026: the double. Series E at $48B; run-rate revenue "near $900M" per the company (Forbes flags that Cognition has not disclosed how it computes run-rate). The Information (via TechCrunch) projects $4-5B annualized by end of 2026, against Cursor's $6B+ trajectory.

The argument map

  • Thesis: automating software itself accelerates every industry, and demand is infinite, so engineers gain leverage instead of losing jobs.
  • Sub-claim 1: agents already write most code at leading companies (13% to 90%+ at Cognition).
  • Sub-claim 2: leverage multiplies: 10x to 100x engineer, 1000x coming; bricklayers become architects.
  • Sub-claim 3: history refutes the doomers - compilers, open source, and cloud all "shrank the profession" into explosive growth instead.
  • Objection 1 (conflict of interest): every number is the vendor's or its customers'; the essay is the funding announcement.
  • Objection 2 (undefined metrics): "production code" and "run-rate revenue" are both self-defined and method-undisclosed.
  • Objection 3 (the ratio problem): infinite demand for software is not infinite demand for software engineers - if one architect directs 100x output, you need fewer architects.
  • Objection 4 (the evidence gap): the one randomized controlled trial of experienced developers using AI tools (METR, July 2025) found them 19% slower - while believing they were 20% faster. Vendor case studies vs an RCT is the evidentiary shape of this whole debate.

How the venues see it

On X the essay splits cleanly by network: the investor/founder layer congratulates (Scott Wu, a16z partners, Sheetal Jaitly: "Marc has updated his thesis!") while the unaffiliated reply layer mocks the metaphors and names the conflict of interest within hours. HN - the venue that would actually interrogate "90% of production code" (define code, define wrote, who reviews the diff) - has no thread yet. If one comes, expect the top comment to be a demand for the metric's methodology, because HN has fought this exact fight over every "% of code is AI" claim since 2024. The gap is the opportunity: the methodology question is currently unclaimed on X.

Key facts, prodded

  • 31.5% to 94.4% of top-10 market cap: directionally true and checkable (the top-10 is now nearly all tech) - but top-10 composition is a cherry-pickable frame; it measures concentration, not software's total economic share.
  • 13% to 90%+ of production code: self-reported by Cognition about its own repo, no methodology. Lines? Commits? Tokens? Reviewed-and-merged vs generated? The number is doing the essay's heaviest lifting and is the least checkable. For calibration, Anthropic's analogous internal figure does have a disclosed methodology: leadership has quoted "90%+" when counting scripts, while the stricter lines-merged-to-production measure read >80% in May 2026 (Anthropic Institute, June). The ~10-point gap between two definitions of the same claim is exactly the ambiguity Cognition's number hides.
  • Mercedes 8 months to 8 days, Rivian 10x, Itau 70%: customer case studies, the classic vendor metric genre. Real deployments, unknowable baselines.
  • $492M to ~$900M run-rate in ~4 months: company-disclosed, method undisclosed (Forbes). The Information's $4-5B year-end projection implies another ~5x. Revenue this size is hard to fake entirely, easy to round generously.
  • The infinite-demand argument: Jevons-flavored and genuinely contested when applied to labor rather than output - history's compilers/cloud examples automated tasks, not the task-creator.
  • The a16z boomerang: exited Cursor via the SpaceX sale, then led a $2B round in Cursor's direct rival weeks later. Conviction in the category, agnosticism about the horse - or the reverse.

Both sides, taken seriously

"The thesis is right, and the COI doesn't change it"

Steelman

The 2011 essay was mocked too, and its market-cap scoreboard is the cleanest public vindication any tech thesis has ever had. The new claims are the kind an investor with access actually can check: a16z saw Cognition's repos, customers, and revenue before wiring $2B at double the price in four months - costly signaling, not vibes. Everyone who works at a serious engineering org in 2026 knows agents write most of the new code; the essay just says it out loud with numbers attached. And the Friedman point stands: every previous automation panic confused a task dying with a profession dying.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"Number go up, pmarca is always right." Track record is not evidence about this metric, and the 90% claim still has no published methodology.

"This is a pitch deck wearing an essay"

Steelman

Every load-bearing number is vendor-sourced or self-defined: production-code percentage, run-rate revenue, the customer case studies. The one randomized trial that exists measured experienced devs getting slower while feeling faster - which is exactly what a hype cycle feels like from inside. The labor argument commits the switcheroo: infinite demand for software says nothing about demand for software engineers, and the bricklayers-to-architects metaphor quietly concedes it (buildings need few architects). And the structure gives it away: thesis, portfolio company, "I know where I'm putting my money." It is an announcement - a good one - and should be priced as one.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"AI coding is fake, Devin doesn't work." The revenue and the customer list are real; the argument is about what the numbers mean, not whether they exist.
