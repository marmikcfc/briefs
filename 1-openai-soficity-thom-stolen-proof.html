"OpenAI might have stolen another major proof" - a top sofic-groups mathematician says OpenAI denied using his ChatGPT conversations, and OpenAI's own later carve-out makes that denial look misleading

September 10, 2026, ~12:30 PM IST · Valerio Capraro's BREAKING post is ~10h old: 593K views, 11K likes, 3.2K reposts, 402 replies · HN 49638353 front-page now, ~3h old, 107pts / 23 comments · Andreas Thom's three mathstodon posts are ~21h old · no response from OpenAI, Bubeck, or Sellke to Thom yet · blow-up: very high (third scoop accusation in a week, named mathematician, email receipts, and a live "the evidence is thin" counter on HN)
The gistAndreas Thom (TU Dresden, a leading expert on sofic and hyperlinear groups, twenty years on the problem) says he and Gabor Kun spent months discussing their approach to Gromov's soficity conjecture with ChatGPT - and when OpenAI later announced its Astra model had found a non-sofic group using Kun and Thom's methods, Thom emailed OpenAI's Mark Sellke and Sebastien Bubeck asking two explicit questions: did our conversations enter training data, and were they accessible to the solving process? Sellke's complete answer: "Regarding your conversations with ChatGPT: that did not happen." Thom's charge, posted to mathstodon yesterday and amplified by Valerio Capraro's viral BREAKING post today: OpenAI's own statement in the Buckmaster-Alpoge case - that it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models" - means Sellke's categorical denial could only have covered direct access, a distinction Thom's question explicitly made and the answer erased. Thom calls it "plainly dishonest." Capraro: after Alpoge, Buckmaster, and now Thom, "we may be looking at one of the greatest intellectual scandals in the history of science." HN's top counter: Thom never claims they had a proof - only that they were working on one - so "stolen proof" overstates even his own account.

What happened

Thom posted a three-part mathstodon thread (Sep 9, ~3:40 PM IST) laying out his evidence: his email to Sellke and Bubeck after the non-sofic-group announcement, Sellke's one-line categorical denial, and the contrast with OpenAI's newer, hedged language in the Buckmaster-Alpoge case. He adds a circumstantial mathematical point: the Kun-Thom route was not the main line of attack on non-soficity (quantum-games approaches looked more promising), so the model taking exactly that route raised his suspicion. He also notes he disabled model training on June 29 - a control he calls prospective and unauditable. Capraro (a mathematics professor who knows Thom) posted the accusation to X at ~2:30 AM ET with the "greatest intellectual scandal" framing; it passed 593K views in ten hours and hit the HN front page around 9:50 AM IST.

How we got here

  1. The math: Gromov's soficity conjecture (2000) asks whether every group is "sofic" - approximable by finite structures. Thom and Kun are among the world's leading specialists; a non-sofic group would settle it in the unexpected direction.
  2. OpenAI's announcement: among the ten problems OpenAI said Astra solved was a non-sofic group - found, per the announcement itself, using the methods of Kun and Thom.
  3. The Alpoge episode (early Sep): mathematician Levent Alpoge accused OpenAI of authorship pressure around its Navier-Stokes proof; Bubeck denied "false and inflammatory allegations" (Sep 8) and said coordination was attempted. See the Buckmaster brief.
  4. The carve-out (this week): in the Buckmaster-Alpoge response, OpenAI said no specific user data was accessed but it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models" - the sentence Thom's whole argument now leans on.
  5. Tao's frame (this week): Terence Tao warned that even a rumor of AI progress on a problem now triggers a swarm, and proposed norms where raw AI solutions carry "negligible or even negative value" - see the Tao brief.
  6. Sep 9-10: Thom's mathstodon thread, then Capraro's post, then HN.

The argument map

  • Accusation: unpublished human work discussed with ChatGPT was absorbed into the model and presented as the model's own breakthrough; OpenAI's categorical denial was materially misleading given its own de-identified-data carve-out.
  • Evidence for: the email exchange (a real categorical denial on record); the carve-out contradiction; the circumstantial route argument (the model took a non-mainline approach); a pattern - Alpoge, Buckmaster, Thom in one week.
  • Evidence against: Thom does not claim a completed proof was taken, only months of discussions about an approach; the Kun-Thom methods were published and public, so the route was learnable without any chat logs; Sellke's "that did not happen" may have been a good-faith answer about direct access, not a lawyerly dodge; convergent discovery is the norm once a field knows a result is reachable.
  • The deeper fight 1 (consent): even if legal, is training on users' unpublished research discussions and then racing those users to publication "ethically indefensible" (Thom) or just the terms of service working as written?
  • The deeper fight 2 (de-identification): "de-identification removes a name; it does not remove the intellectual content of a mathematical idea" - whether anonymized-use carve-outs answer the moral question at all.

The asymmetry matters for a reply: the gap between Capraro's headline and Thom's own careful account is visible from the HN side and mostly invisible on X.

Key facts, prodded

  • The email denial is the strongest card. Sellke's complete answer to an explicit two-part question was "that did not happen." Against OpenAI's own later "cannot rule out de-identified data derived from usage" language, the denial reads as either carefully scoped (direct access only) or wrong. Either reading is bad: one is misleading a questioner who drew the distinction explicitly, the other is false.
  • What Thom does NOT claim: that he and Kun had a proof. His account is months of approach discussions plus a circumstantial route argument. "Stolen proof" is Capraro's headline, not Thom's - HN noticed within the hour.
  • The route argument is real but weak: the Kun-Thom methods were published; a capable model could find that route from the public literature. It raises the prior, it doesn't carry the case.
  • The June 29 opt-out: Thom disabled training then; the control is prospective and doesn't answer what happened to earlier conversations - and OpenAI's answer reportedly never mentioned any account-specific check.
  • What would settle it: exactly what Thom asks for - the basis of the denial (settings checked, datasets, checkpoints). Only OpenAI holds the logs; the accusers cannot reverse-engineer the pipeline.
  • The pattern cut both ways: three accusations in a week is either a scandal surfacing or a community whose incentive now runs the other way (being scooped-by-AI is a reputation-preserving explanation for losing a race). Both readings are live in the discourse.

Both sides, taken seriously

"This is a scandal, and the denial is the proof"

Steelman

Strip the headline down to Thom's own document and it is still damning: a researcher asked a precise two-part question about his own unpublished work, got a one-line categorical denial, and the company's later public language reveals the denial could not honestly have covered the training-data half. Add the circumstantial route, the prospective-only opt-out, and two other named mathematicians in the same week, and you get the shape of institutional behavior: absorb quietly, deny broadly, hedge later. De-identification answers a legal question, not the moral one - the ideas are the identity.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"OpenAI definitely trained on Thom's chats and Astra just replayed them." No direct evidence of that exists publicly, and Thom himself doesn't claim a finished proof was taken.

"This is grief wearing a wiretap"

Steelman

Everything public is consistent with a much duller story: the Kun-Thom methods were in the literature for years; once OpenAI's models got strong enough, the reachable result fell - the same week independent groups (Anima on Navier-Stokes) fell into the same territory, which is what convergent discovery looks like. Sellke's "that did not happen" plausibly meant "nobody accessed your conversations," which may simply be true, and the later carve-out is lawyers describing a training pipeline, not confessing to this case. The accusers cannot lose: any AI progress on any problem anyone discussed with ChatGPT now counts as evidence of theft.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"Mathematicians are just coping." Thom's email and Sellke's reply are real documents; the contradiction with the carve-out language deserves an answer regardless of motive.
