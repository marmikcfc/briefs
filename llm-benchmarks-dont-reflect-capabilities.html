"I don't think there's any benchmarks that accurately reflect the current capabilities of LLMs"

September 5, 2026 · candidate source: X (@theo, 385K followers) + Hacker News front page · blow-up: high (122K views, 260 replies, and HN is litigating the same question at the same time)

The story

Theo (t3.gg, CEO of T3 Chat, 385K followers) posted a one-line claim: "I don't think there's any benchmarks that accurately reflect the current capabilities of LLMs" (Sep 4, 121.8K views, 260 replies, 3.3K likes). On the same morning, Hacker News was chewing on the exact same question from the other side: Artificial Analysis released Intelligence Index v4.2, and the HN thread (103 pts) immediately accused the index-makers of retuning their benchmark so GPT-6 Astra would rank the way social media expected. A third thread, "Can AI design circuit boards yet?" (eebench, 103 pts), showed a domain benchmark where GPT-5.6 Sol scores below GPT-5.5 - the only benchmark showing a regression on a new model. The measurement layer of the AI industry is having a legitimacy crisis in public, today.

Why it invites an opinion

Everyone who builds with these models has a stake: if benchmarks don't track capability, then model selection, procurement, and "X beats Y" launch marketing are all running on vibes. The disagreement is not about whether models are good - it's about whether the numbers mean anything, and who the numbers serve.

On X, Theo's claim drew mostly agreement and jokes ("t3bench incoming?", "solve cancer, that's the benchmark", "@bijanbowen's videos are all the benchmark I need"). The one hostile quote accused Theo of being paid by OpenAI and Anthropic - engagement bait, not argument. X is venting the feeling; HN is auditing the instrument.

Both sides

"Theo is right"

Steelman

Aggregate scores compress a jagged frontier into a single number that no working engineer can act on; public test sets are trained on within weeks; and the moment a benchmark drives marketing, Goodhart's law eats it. The proof is in the index-makers' own behavior: saturation retirements, private sets, and emergency retunes when the ranking offends lived experience. Practitioners (Buoylog, Rook, rpcope1's PCB account) all report the same thing: the number and the experience diverge.

Strawman (what the other side hears)

"Benchmarks are fake, trust vibes" - which conveniently unfalsifiable, always available to whoever's model just lost, and usually ends with the complainer launching their own benchmark.

"Theo overreaches"

Steelman

"No benchmark accurately reflects capabilities" is too strong. Domain and behavioral evals with published variance (eebench), refusal-aware axes (Omniscience), and contamination-resistant private sets DO track real differences - eebench caught a Sol regression that vibes alone would have missed, and kingstnap's fix (pre-committed reanalysis cadence) addresses retuning bias without throwing out measurement. The alternative to imperfect benchmarks isn't truth; it's marketing.

Strawman (what the other side hears)

"The index is fine, keep shopping by the leaderboard" - which the index-makers themselves don't believe, or they wouldn't be hiding 40% of it.
