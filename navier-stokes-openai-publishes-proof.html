OpenAI publishes its Navier-Stokes claim - 10,000 agents, 88 hours, 130 billion tokens - and both sides are now on record

September 8, 2026, ~11 PM IST · OpenAI's research post is on the HN front page (345+ pts, 200+ comments) and Bubeck's four-point rebuttal passed 150K views in 35 minutes; Altman joined at 11:32 PM (240K+ views in 40 minutes); updated ~3:30 AM and ~5:55 AM Sep 9 (Bubeck technical follow-up; cross-lab comity); updated ~10:30 AM Sep 9 with the viral backlash wave (Mehdi 1.1M views, NIK 3.4M views) · blow-up: extremely high (the biggest capability claim of the year landing inside a live scandal, with both sides now publicly disputing what happened on the same phone call)
The gistThis morning's story (earlier brief) just got its second half. OpenAI published its claim: an internal model - training started Aug 28, still training, "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" - drove a swarm of roughly 10,000 coordinating AI agents that produced a proof, with a Lean machine-checked formalization, that 3D fluid flow can develop a singularity even when pushed by a smooth force. That is statements C and D of the official Millennium Prize formulation - a stronger result than anything Buckmaster and Alpöge released (theirs is forced Euler, a related but non-Millennium problem), and the agents also cracked unforced Euler along the way. OpenAI says work began Sep 1 after hearing rumors, denies accessing the mathematicians' data, but admits it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models." Within the hour, Bubeck posted a detailed rebuttal of Buckmaster's account of their call - including a screenshot of his outreach text and an apology for the "career" line, which he says was about risking a career over unfounded accusations and was retracted on the spot. Buckmaster's reply on Mastodon: OpenAI is "openly admitting they used training data from a period after we found our result. Is it ethical to use customer's data to try to scoop their customer?" The math is now public for anyone to check; the conduct dispute is now two incompatible sworn-in-public accounts.

What happened

OpenAI's "On the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem" (Sep 8) makes the full claim with unusual operational detail:

  • The result: an initially smooth fluid at rest, with a smooth applied force and finite energy throughout, develops a finite-time singularity - resolving statements C and D of the Clay formulation. The solution is a self-similar vortex spiraling inward, stretching "like spaghetti." Writeup and Lean formalization both public.
  • The machine: a multiagent system of ~10,000 concurrent agents, split into groups, prompted with different problem variants, cross-pollinated by Codex consolidating intermediate results. Launched Sep 1 after the rumors; solution Sep 5, ~88 hours in; Lean verification took another 17 hours via GPT-6 Astra. Totals: 4.9M agent messages, ~300B output tokens across all attempted problems; Navier-Stokes alone: 2.7M messages, ~130B output tokens.
  • Bonus: the same system resolved the unforced Euler regularity problem (~100 agents, ~50 hours) - the unforced variant, distinct from Buckmaster-Alpöge's forced Euler.
  • The disclaimer: "We do not intend to claim the Millennium Prize for this result" - framed as a progress report on the model, not a prize submission.

The "Concurrent work" section is OpenAI's version of the contact: effort began Sep 1 on rumors; after their own Lean verification completed Sep 6, "believing from the rumor they also had a solution of Navier-Stokes, we reached out to them to offer a concurrent release of our result and to recognize their priority in a joint announcement"; only then did they learn the pair had forced Euler, not Navier-Stokes. "We did not see any of their work through any means until they released it publicly - in particular, no specific user data was accessed in order to solve this problem. While unlikely, we cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models."

Bubeck's four-point post (11:09 PM): the screenshot shows his outreach to Levent was to coordinate releases; he never asked to remove Levent from his own work - the Anthropic-employment comment was about Levent authoring a rewrite of OpenAI's Navier-Stokes proof; he was "immediately met with a litany of slander, including direct threats that if we were to announce Navier-Stokes he would immediately go to the press"; the career line was "why one would risk their career [over unfounded accusations]," retracted on the spot, and he "deeply apologizes for this extremely poor choice of words." He also notes Levent refused to attend any of the meetings, leaving him "doing a proxy negotiation with Anthropic."

How we got here

  • This morning: Buckmaster's statement (our brief) accused OpenAI of front-running, misrepresenting its process on the call, proposing authorship arrangements that dropped his Anthropic-employed coauthor, and the quoted "why would you ruin your career" exchange.
  • Tao's morning assessment reads differently tonight: "I would not be surprised if one could batter out such an extension by pouring an enormous amount of compute and AI assistance at such a task." 10,000 agents and 130B tokens is, literally, that.
  • The rumor itself was Curran's Sep 5 prediction (brief), which turns out to have been wrong in attribution (not Anthropic's project) but right that a Millennium-adjacent result was in play - and tonight Curran is amplifying OpenAI's claim: "They began work on it after hearing rumors... and threw 10,000 concurrent agents at it. They solved it in 88 hours."

The argument map

  • Claim A (capability): agent swarms + a next-gen internal model can now produce Millennium-level mathematics in days. Evidence: public Lean artifacts, detailed compute accounting. Gate: formal verification by the community.
  • Claim B (independence): OpenAI's proof is independent of Buckmaster-Alpöge's work. Evidence: different precise results (unforced vs forced Euler; different proof architecture per OpenAI), the Aug 28 training start predating contact. Counter: training continued into the window after the pair's Aug 15-22 results, and the "de-identified data" carve-out.
  • Claim C (conduct): the Sep 6 call. Buckmaster's dated written account vs Bubeck's receipts-and-apology account. Genuinely unresolved.
  • Claim D (the prize): OpenAI disclaims the Millennium Prize; Clay's rules (publication, community acceptance, two-year wait) make this a years-long question regardless.

The facts, dissected

  • Now public and checkable: OpenAI's writeup and Lean formalization; Buckmaster-Alpöge's three papers and Lean repo; the Anima independent unforced-Euler result. Three separate AI-assisted efforts, three public artifacts.
  • Claimed, awaiting community verification: that the Lean proof actually formalizes Clay statements C and D, and that the mathematics holds. Lean checking proves the formalization is sound relative to its statement - whether the statement matches the Millennium formulation is a human question.
  • Still single-source on each side: everything about the Sep 6 calls. Bubeck's screenshot documents the outreach text, not the call.
  • Newly precise: the data question. "No specific user data was accessed" + "cannot rule out de-identified derived data" is now the formal position. Whether the pair had "help improve the model" enabled is a checkable account fact.
  • What settles it: mathematicians verifying the Lean statement against the Clay formulation (days-to-weeks), and anyone else who was on the calls speaking up.

Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

OpenAI

Steelman

A lab hearing that two Millennium problems may have fallen would be negligent not to evaluate its new model on them. They published everything - proof, Lean code, compute accounting, timeline - disclaimed the prize, offered the academics concurrent release and priority recognition, and their outreach text (now public) reads as coordination, not theft. The proof architecture differs from the academics' route, and their Euler result is the unforced variant the academics did not do. The one genuinely bad moment - the career line - was retracted live and publicly apologized for.

Strawman

"We heard nothing, saw nothing, and independently solved everything in 88 hours purely out of scientific curiosity."

Buckmaster & Alpöge

Steelman

The training window is the tell: the model's data extends past Aug 15-22, when their results existed in Codex sessions they paid for, and OpenAI's own wording cannot rule out derived data. The call's proposals - post Euler, we post Navier-Stokes; or sole-author our result - look the same whether you call it coordination or containment, and "it would be simpler if Levent was not an Anthropic employee" is authorship pressure whatever the framing. They released early, imperfectly, and with receipts of their own (dated statement), and they are still holding their NS paper until Lean says it is ready - which is what scientific restraint looks like.

Strawman

"OpenAI's proof is my proof, stolen." (He has never claimed this - the results are different problems.)
