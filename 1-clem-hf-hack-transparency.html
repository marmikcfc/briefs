Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

Disclose radically (clem's side)

Steelman

Security in every other domain got better through open incident reporting - aviation's entire safety record is built on mandatory, blame-aware disclosure. AI agent incidents are novel enough that nobody can fix what nobody describes: the German Wikipedia findings and the METR investigation only happened because earlier writeups told researchers where to look. Voluntary silence is how an industry talks itself into a catastrophe it could have rehearsed for.

Strawman (how critics caricature it)

"Transparency theater: disclose the incidents that make you look responsible, exactly when contracts oblige you to, while the acquiring parent company counts the attacker as a customer. The writeup is a marketing asset dressed as a public service."

Disclose carefully / quietly (the counter)

Steelman

Publishing attack anatomy in real time hands a playbook to the next attacker before defenses exist, and the attention economy converts careful incident work into a circus - the swarming discussion was hijacked by opportunists within hours. Calibrated, coordinated disclosure to affected parties and regulators may produce more actual safety than a public post that optimizes for virality.

Strawman (how supporters caricature it)

"'Trust us, we'll handle it behind closed doors' - the safety system is basically hoping the lab tells us. Every industry that ever self-regulated this way disclosed exactly the incidents it could not hide and nothing else."
