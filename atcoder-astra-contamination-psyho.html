The man who beat the AI says the AI now wins 40 of 43 - and AtCoder's coordinator has a contamination smoking gun for Opus 5

September 7-8, 2026 · candidate source: Psyho amplifying AtCoder problem coordinator wata's experiment (Psyho's quote 12h old, 148 likes, 38K views; wata's originals 15-17h) · blow-up: medium-high (the exact authorities on humans-vs-AI contests, making a falsifiable capability claim plus an evidence-backed contamination accusation)
The gistAtCoder runs heuristic contests - four-hour optimization problems where there is no perfect answer, only better ones, and where humanity's champion Psyho famously beat OpenAI's AI last year ("Humanity has prevailed (for now!)"). Yoichi Iwata (wata), the coordinator who designs those problems, ran models offline against every short contest: with Codex driving improvement loops, the AI would have taken 1st place in 40 of 43. But the sharper finding is about Claude Opus 5: it averaged 3rd place on older contests and collapsed to 39th-153rd on recent ones - the signature of a model that memorized the old problems rather than learning to solve them. Astra shows no such drop, which wata calls "unclear" - either genuinely better, or better at hiding it. Psyho amplified all of it to the English-speaking world, with his own caveat: if web search was on, even these numbers are hard to interpret.

How we got here

  • AtCoder's AHC is the sacred ground of humans-vs-AI. In July 2025 Psyho beat OpenAI's entrant in a marathon heuristic contest - "Humanity has prevailed (for now!)" ran to 2.2M views and made him the face of human resistance. When he amplifies a result, the community treats it as settled that he has no incentive to oversell the machine.
  • ALE-Bench (Sakana AI) already tracks this. The leaderboard scores models on AtCoder heuristic problems; wata's experiment extends it to contests too new to be in the benchmark - which is precisely what makes the contamination comparison possible.
  • The contamination playbook is well understood. Train on public problems, score brilliantly on them, collapse on fresh ones. The astra-spectrogram debate (Curran brief) is the same fight in another modality: is the capability real, or is the test in the training set?
  • Contest hygiene is now a live policy question. AHC070 was run AI-prohibited; wata checked Opus 5's equivalent rank anyway (36th) to rule out "the humans got better AI too" as the explanation for the drop.

What's new today (monitoring)

  1. The Opus 5 drop-off has a control condition. wata checked AHC070 (AI-prohibited, so human scores are clean) and Opus 5 still ranked ~36th-equivalent - so the collapse on recent contests is not "everyone else used better AI." That is what elevates this from suspicion to evidence: the alternative explanation got tested and excluded.
  2. The harness is boring, which makes it scarier. Asked if special prompts or toolsets drove the result, wata: "Nothing particularly special; offline with the problem statements and tools downloaded locally. 'Examine a solution approach likely to get 1st place, implement it in C++, contest time 4 hours, keep iterating.'" No scaffolding magic - the base capability plus a generic loop is the whole recipe. That undercuts the "it's the harness, not the model" defense before anyone makes it.
  3. Humans still won three - and the losses are diagnostic. wata's list: AHC015 (the AI trained a neural net and timed out), AHC064 and AHC065 (couldn't reach the intended solution shape). The failure mode is engineering judgment under time pressure, not reasoning depth - which is also why wata's AHC015 fix story (astra diagnosed a denormalized-number TLE, one fix, 1st place) is the detail practitioners are bookmarking.
  4. wata's fresh coda reframes the whole thing. 44 minutes ago: he hopes for "an AHC world's Shota Fujii - someone who studies AI's solution methods and acquires that superhuman power." The coordinator of the contests is not mourning; he is scouting the first human to train ON the machines. That is the take nobody else is making.

  • Contamination claim (wata): Opus 5's collapse on post-cutoff contests (3rd average to 39th-153rd) indicates explicit training on the older problems.

  • Caveat B (implicit): "ignoring the 4-hour rule" means overtime runs; contest conditions were not fully respected (AHC015's TLE-in-overtime note).

  • Forward claim (wata): the next human champion studies the AI's methods, not just the problems.

The facts, dissected

  • 40/43 is "ignoring the 4-hour rule." The loops ran overtime. Under contest conditions the count is lower (AHC015's fix took overtime; mid-run it placed equivalent-3rd). The claim is about ceiling, not regulation play.
  • The contamination signal is a difference-in-differences: old contests (likely in training data) - strong; new contests (too fresh) - collapsed; AI-prohibited contest control - still weak. Each leg is individually checkable.
  • "Suggests" is doing honest work. wata says the drop-off suggests explicit training; nobody outside Anthropic can confirm it. But the pattern is the textbook signature, and the control kills the cleanest alternative.
  • Astra's "unclear" cuts both ways: no drop-off is consistent with genuine generalization - and with contamination that generalizes better. Only a private-problem eval settles it.
  • Web search is the uncontrolled variable. If enabled, models could read public solution repos; Psyho flagged it immediately. wata says runs were offline with local tools, but the repo question is not fully closed.
