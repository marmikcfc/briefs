"Functionally indistinguishable from AGI" - Amjad Masad says the relentless programmer is already here, and coding problems are "virtually solved"

September 7, 2026 · candidate source: Amjad Masad post (1h, 15.6K views, 47 replies - fresh, inside the 6h ideal window) · blow-up: medium-high (Replit's CEO planting the third flag of the weekend's AGI triangle - directly against Casado's "coding has saturated" from last night)
The gistAmjad Masad - Replit's CEO, the man whose company sells AI coding to millions - posted: "I don't think we've reached AGI but what we have is functionally indistinguishable from AGI. Because we have a relentless programmer that doesn't get bored or tired. So any problem that can be casted as a coding problem is virtually solved." That is three separate claims stacked in three lines, and the replies are pulling at all of them. It also flatly contradicts what a16z's Martin Casado said 19 hours earlier - that coding has saturated and his private test set stopped moving. One of them is describing the field wrong, and both sell picks-and-shovels to it. When asked what would count as real AGI, Amjad answered: AIs that can learn on the fly.

What happened

At 5:48 PM IST, Masad posted the claim with an image attached. In the replies he defined his missing ingredient: the AGI moment is when "you'd be interacting with AIs that can learn on the fly" - systems that update themselves from experience instead of waiting for the next training run. In plain terms: today's AI, he says, never gets smarter during your conversation; it only seems tireless. True AGI, for him, keeps learning while you use it.

47 replies in the first hour. The thread is a live definitional brawl, but one reply does the real work: "Coding got cheap. Judging which problem deserved to be cast that way didn't."

How we got here

  • This is the third vertex of the weekend's AGI triangle. Saturday: Jensen Huang declared "AGI has arrived" over GPT-6 Astra (the Jensen brief). Sunday night: Casado countered that coding has saturated (the Casado brief). Monday evening: Amjad splits the difference - not AGI, but functionally AGI, and coding is solved. Three heavyweight claims, none compatible with the other two.
  • Amjad talks his book, and the book is relevant. Replit's entire business is "any problem castable as coding." If that class is virtually solved, Replit's agent is the delivery vehicle. That does not make him wrong; it makes the claim load-bearing for his company, which is exactly why it is worth stress-testing.
  • "Learn on the fly" is the field's actual live debate. Continual learning - models updating from experience - is the capability every lab is chasing and none has shipped. Amjad naming it as his AGI bar puts him closer to the skeptics than his headline suggests.
  • The definition fight is the tell. Four replies in an hour are pure definition warfare ("AGI is an empty conceptual designation," "we did reach AGI, it's Another GPT Intelligence"). When nobody can define the term, every claim about it is really a claim about what the speaker is selling or fearing.

What's new today (monitoring)

  1. The sharpest counter is about problem selection, not coding. Radu Stefanescu: "Maybe. Coding got cheap. Judging which problem deserved to be cast that way didn't." This reframes the whole claim: the bottleneck moved from execution to judgment - which is the same conclusion as the Paras Chopra thread two hours earlier, reached from the opposite direction. If unmeasured means unprotected (Chopra) and problem-choice is the scarce skill (Stefanescu), then "virtually solved" describes the easy half of the job.
  2. Amjad's own AGI bar undercuts his headline. Asked what the real AGI moment looks like, he said "AIs that can learn on the fly" - which today's models cannot do. By his own definition, the relentless programmer cannot improve from your corrections. Every experienced agent user has felt exactly that wall; it is the same wall Theo's memory debate and the skills-files thread are trying to route around with files.
  3. The thread is still small enough to matter in. 15.6K views, 47 replies, growing by the minute, with Amjad actively answering. A well-aimed reply in the next hour lands in a live conversation the poster is still reading.

The argument map

  • Claim 1: current AI is functionally indistinguishable from AGI.
  • Claim 2: the mechanism is the tireless programmer - no boredom, no fatigue.
  • Claim 3 (the strong one): any problem castable as coding is virtually solved.
  • His own exception: no learning on the fly - so "functional AGI" excludes adaptation.
  • Counter A (Stefanescu): casting the problem is the hard part, and it did not get cheap.
  • Counter B (Casado, yesterday): the coding itself stopped improving on real work.
  • Counter C (the deflationists): "AGI" carries no content, so the claim is unfalsifiable as stated.
  • Open question: is "virtually solved" a capability statement or an economics statement - and whose judgement prices the casting?

The details that matter

  • "Virtually solved" is doing all the work. Casting a problem as coding means specifying it precisely enough for a programmer - tireless or not. The casting is the judgment, and it is exactly what Chopra's free-variable thread says agents cannot be trusted with unaided.
  • The three weekend claims cannot all be true. Jensen: AGI arrived. Casado: coding saturated. Amjad: not AGI, but coding solved. The disagreement is not noise - it locates the real fault line: capability on new surfaces vs reliability on existing work.
  • "Learn on the fly" dates the claim. If continual learning ships in a future model, Amjad's exception dissolves and his "functional AGI" becomes full AGI by his own definition. It is a falsifiable bet with a visible tripwire.
  • Who sells what. Jensen sells compute, Casado invests in infrastructure, Amjad sells coding agents. Each man's AGI claim matches his inventory. That does not refute any of them; it tells you which evidence each one watches.

The strongest case for each side

"Functionally AGI is honest and important"

SteelmanTirelessness is genuinely new in economic history. A worker who never sleeps, never gets bored, and costs pennies per hour changes what problems are worth attempting at all - most problems never get cast as coding because the attempt was too expensive, and that constraint just fell. Amjad's hedge (no learning on the fly) shows he is measuring, not hyping.

Strawman (the weak version to avoid)"AGI is here, bow to the machine god." He explicitly did not say that, and the thread's dunk attempts pretending he did are easy to swat.

"This is definitional fog from a seller"

Steelman"Functionally indistinguishable from AGI" unfalsifiably borrows AGI's aura while disowning its tests. Casado's test set - actual unsolved issues, thrown at every new model - stopped moving; that is measurement, and it contradicts "virtually solved" on the exact surface Amjad claims. And the tireless programmer still needs a tireless specifier: the casting judgment did not get cheaper, as the thread's best reply notes.

Strawman (the weak version to avoid)"Amjad is just shilling Replit." The incentive is real but the claim survives it - Replit's usage numbers are not the question; whether casting or coding is the bottleneck is.
