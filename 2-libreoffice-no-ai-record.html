"No AI" broke LibreOffice's download record - and the top comments say AI did it

September 8, 2026 on the HN front page (327+ pts, 100+ comments) · candidate source: Manual do Usuário writeup of The Document Foundation's "no AI is now a feature" push · blow-up: medium (a feel-good anti-hype claim being falsified in real time by its own comment section, with a genuinely interesting structural story underneath)
The gistLibreOffice, the free open-source office suite, has been marketing itself as the office suite with no built-in AI - "no AI is now a feature," its foundation blogged on Sep 3. A writeup claims downloads just broke records because of that stance, and HN ran it to the front page. The top comments puncture the story two ways: the download numbers have been roughly flat since 2019 ("Unix timestamp counter breaks record after writing this comment"), and Simon Willison noticed on Sep 2 that OpenAI's desktop app quietly bundles a full copy of LibreOffice - meaning a chunk of those "record downloads" may be AI agents, not AI-weary humans. Underneath the irony is a real structural observation, made by Mat Velloso and others: AI agents are standardizing on open-source tools they can script freely, and that may matter far more than whether the suite has a chatbot in it.

What happened

The Document Foundation declared "no AI is now a feature" on Sep 3, continuing a positioning that started with the 26.8 release notes ("And no built-in AI, of course"). Manual do Usuário then framed the latest download record as a verdict: users are rewarding the anti-AI stance. HN's thread is where the claim met the data.

How we got here

  • Every major suite shipped AI this cycle. Microsoft Copilot in Office, Gemini in Workspace - the default office experience now includes an assistant. LibreOffice chose the opposite lane, explicitly.
  • Simon Willison's Sep 2 find: the ChatGPT desktop app (formerly Codex) bundles the entire LibreOffice suite in a hidden cache folder for MS Office compatibility. Agents don't need Word licenses; they need a scriptable converter.
  • The anti-AI marketing wave. LibreOffice is not alone in selling absence; the bet is that AI fatigue is a market segment. The record-downloads claim is the first big "proof point" - which is why the falsification matters.

What's new today (monitoring)

  1. The record is real but the attribution is contested. HN's Aurornis pulled the foundation's own stats: monthly downloads mostly flat since 2019; "record-breaking" weeks are a rising-tide artifact. The story's engine - "users are fleeing AI" - currently has no clean data behind it.
  2. The agent-bundling confounder is live. reilly3000 and jgbuddy in the thread: Codex/ChatGPT desktop installs pull a LibreOffice copy each. Nobody has published what fraction of downloads that is.
  3. The more durable claim surfaced downstream. Velloso: "all your AI agents use LibreOffice rather than Microsoft Office APIs. The pattern will repeat." Tworek's version: open source gets trained-on and optimized-for for free - "Blender may have just won as a 3D asset creation software." This is the argument that outlives the download chart: agents pick tools they can operate, and open formats win by default.

The argument map

  • Claim A (the headline): "No AI" marketing caused record downloads. Evidence: timing, foundation messaging. Counter: flat monthly stats since 2019; bundling confounder.
  • Claim B (the rebuttal): the record is noise plus agent installs - AI caused it, not anti-AI. Evidence: simonw's find, stats graph. Counter: bundle installs may not register as downloads at all (cache copies vs. download counter - unverified).
  • Claim C (the interesting one): regardless of who caused the chart, agents are standardizing on open-source tooling, which quietly shifts platform power away from API-gated incumbents. Evidence: Codex bundling, Blender pattern. Counter: one bundling decision is not a trend; Microsoft can ship agent-friendly APIs if it wants.

Where X and Hacker News diverge

X heard the story a week early and went straight to the structural layer - agents standardizing on open tools (Velloso, Tworek, simonw's bundling find). HN got the marketing frame today and immediately litigated the chart itself: flat-since-2019 data, the meaninglessness of "record" weeks, and whether a bundled cache copy even counts as a download. X asks "who wins the agent era"; HN asks "is this graph lying." Both are poking the same hole from different sides: the download number cannot carry the story being hung on it.

The facts, dissected

  • Verified: the foundation's no-AI positioning is official and repeated (26.8 notes, Sep 3 blog); the ChatGPT desktop app does bundle LibreOffice (simonw, with folder path); the foundation publishes its own download stats.
  • Contested: what the download curve actually shows. The record claim rests on weekly highs; the monthly view is flat since 2019 per HN commenters reading the foundation's own dashboard.
  • Unverified: whether bundled copies pass through the download counter at all - the entire "AI caused the record" joke hinges on this, and nobody in either thread has shown it.
  • What would settle it: the foundation's download telemetry methodology, and OpenAI saying whether the bundle phones the counter.

Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

"No AI" is a real feature, and users are voting

Steelman

AI fatigue is measurable sentiment, privacy-conscious users exist at scale, and a suite that promises your documents never leave your machine is making a legible, honorable product bet. Even if the record-week framing is sloppy, the direction - differentiation by restraint - is a coherent strategy incumbents literally cannot copy without cannibalizing their AI narrative.

Strawman

"Users hate AI so much they're downloading LibreOffice in record numbers" - a claim its own comment section deflated within hours.

The chart is noise; agents are the story

Steelman

The flat monthly series kills the marketing claim, and the bundling find reveals the actual growth driver: AI systems adopting open tools as substrate. LibreOffice's real win is not anti-AI branding - it is becoming the default document engine for agents precisely because it is open, scriptable, and license-free. Restraint on built-in AI and agent-friendliness are the same property seen from two sides: control stays with the operator.

Strawman

"The record is 100% robots" - unproven, and probably not even how the counter works.
