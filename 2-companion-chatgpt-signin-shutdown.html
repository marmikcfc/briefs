How we got here

  • "Sign in with ChatGPT" launched as an identity play. OpenAI's help doc frames it as identity-provider sign-in for participating partners - Airtable, GitLab, HubSpot, Notion, Supabase, Vercel. The doc describes confirming identity, not handing an agent your whole account context.
  • Companion stretched sign-in into delegation. The pitch was not "prove who you are" but "inherit my ChatGPT context and act for me" - a headless Codex instance with an isolated computer, MCPs, and CLIs, driven from iMessage. Reviewers confirm it works well. That is a different product category than the partner list was built for.
  • The account-access gray zone has a history. The same tension ran through the OAuth-wrapping era of third-party ChatGPT clients: platforms want distribution without losing control of account access; builders want the user's existing context without a partnership process. Companion is the loudest test case this month.
  • The founder has been orbiting this space all week. On Sep 6 he shipped sodium.result.dev - "make your website more usable by instinct and every other agent" - quoting WebMCP discussion. Companion's launch dunk on Instinct is a positioning move, not a one-off.

Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

OpenAI is right to gate it

Steelman

Account delegation is not sign-in. An agent inheriting your ChatGPT context can act as you - spend, message, build - and when it goes wrong, users will blame OpenAI, not an 18-year-old's startup. A review gate for that power is the minimum responsible posture, and "OSS clients and partners" is a coherent policy: auditable code or accountable counterparties. Doing it publicly on the launch tweet is how you tell every other builder where the line is before ten more launch.

Strawman (how critics caricature it)

"Congratulations on building something people want on our platform. Unfortunately you did not ask permission first, so we will now correct you in front of your launch traffic and handle the details in private, where our leverage lives."

The builders are right to push through it

Steelman

The user owns their account and explicitly chose to delegate it - informed consent, not circumvention. "Approved use" was never written down for this case, so nothing was clearly violated; the rule was invented at the moment of enforcement, against a product that dunks on the platform's slowness by being fast. If every agent that inherits user context needs a partnership meeting, the agent ecosystem belongs to whoever OpenAI takes meetings with. That is an app-store gate wearing an identity product's clothes.

Strawman (how supporters caricature it)

"Read the terms later, ship the headless account-wrapper now. If the platform objects, that is not a policy - it is a distribution channel: fifty thousand views of martyrdom, courtesy of the landlord."
