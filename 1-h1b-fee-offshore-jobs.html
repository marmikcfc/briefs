The $103,265 H-1B fee may protect a job category by moving the job abroad

September 14, 2026 · Target: HN thread 56 points · 41 comments after 51 minutes

Story summary

DHS has proposed a $103,265 fee for cap-subject H-1B petitions. The intended logic is to make employers exhaust domestic hiring before importing scarce talent. The counter-mechanism is geographic substitution: companies can stop sponsoring a worker without stopping the work, then build the team in Canada, India or Europe.

The rule is not final. It was published August 25, comments close September 24, and DHS must address those comments before a final rule. That distinction matters: this is a live policy design question, not a fee already charged across the program.

How we got here

Chapter 1: an earlier $100K fee collapsed covered demand

A 2025 proclamation raised the fee for certain outside-US applicants. DHS later reported only 85 qualifying petitions through February 15, 2026, an 87% fall from the prior year, and $20 million less H-1B fee revenue.

Chapter 2: courts blocked one route

A federal appeals court upheld a ruling against the proclamation. DHS returned with a proposed rule that would cover the annual cap-subject pool more broadly, including many applicants already in the country.

Chapter 3: firms can substitute locations

IEEE Spectrum contacted 25 US tech firms about their response; none would discuss it on the record. Researchers and hiring advisers describe longer timelines, retention efforts and foreign offices as practical substitutes.

The facts, dissected

Status: proposed rule, not final. Comments close September 24.
Scope: cap-subject H-1B petitions; university, hospital and other nonprofit categories have different treatment.
Prior result: covered petitions fell sharply under the narrower 2025 fee. Unknown: how many jobs were filled by Americans versus moved abroad.
Falsifiable test: compare covered US tech hiring with the same firms' Canada, India and Europe engineering headcount, patenting and team formation over the next year.
Alternative design: salary ranking or an auction could target scarcity and wage quality without a flat $103K barrier, though equity and startup-access problems remain.

Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

Steelman the fee

A scarce visa should not subsidize labor arbitrage. A high fee forces employers to prove the worker's value and reduces dependence on a status that binds people to one sponsor.

Steelman the offshoring critique

Software work crosses borders. If the talent cannot enter, firms can move the role and its supporting team, shrinking US clusters without creating an American hire.

Strawman the fee

“Every foreign engineer steals one American job.” Hiring, team formation and innovation are not fixed-sum one-for-one swaps.

Strawman the critique

“Any visa friction destroys innovation.” The existing program has real worker-power and wage-design flaws worth fixing.
