AI may solve mathematics faster while making the discipline worse at producing understanding

September 12, 2026 · Target: HN thread 72 points · 34 comments after about 1 hour

Story summary

Terence Tao and 24 other Fields Medalists argue that frontier labs are optimizing mathematics for the wrong output. Open problems are not only unanswered questions. They are training grounds where people develop ideas, judgment and a shared language. A lab that mass-produces final proofs can win the benchmark while eroding the process that makes those proofs useful.

The declaration arrives after OpenAI's Navier-Stokes announcement and a bitter priority dispute. Its sharpest claim is broader than plagiarism: solving is a proxy for understanding, and the proxy is becoming the product.

The question is no longer only whether AI can solve a famous problem. It is whether a solved problem still creates human understanding, new questions and new mathematicians.

How we got here

Chapter 1: open problems became lighthouses

Famous conjectures organize research. Partial results produce tools, talks, students and adjacent questions long before a final proof. The community's scarce resource is not merely answers; it is a pipeline that turns hard work into reusable concepts.

Chapter 2: capability outran assimilation

OpenAI says an internal system using 10,000 agents found a formally checked Navier-Stokes blowup proof in 88 hours. Whatever survives scrutiny, frontier systems now search at a speed that can overwhelm the human process of attribution, exposition and teaching.

Chapter 3: a credit dispute exposed the governance gap

Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge had related AI-assisted work in progress. Reporting documented allegations that OpenAI used knowledge of that work to race publication; OpenAI denied misuse. The dispute remains contested, but it showed that normal priority customs were not designed for labs with private models, huge compute and publicity incentives.

What's new today (monitoring)

  • Clay has now spoken, cautiously: CMI says the problem has “apparently been settled,” shares the community’s excitement, and promises an “unhurried” evaluation under its prize rules. The statement avoids naming OpenAI or assigning credit. A fresh HN thread reached 110 points and 53 comments in about three hours, with disagreement centered on whether “apparently” is quiet validation or deliberate institutional neutrality.
  • Twenty-five Fields Medalists signed: this is coordinated resistance from the discipline's highest-status figures, not a lone skeptical essay.
  • The target moved from one scandal to the incentive system: the declaration says benchmark-driven problem solving can damage attribution, pedagogy and the stock of fertile open problems even when the answers are correct.
  • HN split immediately: one camp calls this the clearest description yet of AI hollowing out intellectual work; another sees incumbent protection and asks what practical remedy is possible once capability exists.
  • A concrete follow-up is coming: theorist Shachar Lovett says a two-day Simons working group on similar concerns expects a TCS whitepaper in one or two weeks.
  • The freshest practical proposal is credit reform: commenters predict mathematics must reward conceptual contribution and exposition, not only being first with a proof.

The argument map

Lab objective
Solve famous problems fast and display frontier capability.
→
Visible win
Correct proof, benchmark prestige and investor attention.
Community objective
Grow ideas, people, questions and shared understanding.
→
Hidden loss
Priority races, proof dumps and fewer formative problems.
Possible alignment
Attribution, staged release, exposition and human assimilation.
↔
Hard constraint
Capability cannot realistically be undiscovered.

Where X and Hacker News diverge

X is compressing the story into power and punishment. The timeline's AI discourse is dominated by regulation, lab rivalry and viral moral frames. The subtler distinction between proving and understanding is easy to flatten into “mathematicians fear progress.”

HN is debating institutional design. Commenters ask who assigns credit, whether labs owe a replacement mechanism, whether secrecy becomes rational, and whether understanding can survive after the prestige signal of solving disappears.

The facts, dissected

New fact: CMI now publicly describes Navier-Stokes as “apparently” settled. Meaning: this is a major legitimacy shift from silence, but not final verification, prize recognition, or a credit ruling. The Institute explicitly says evaluation will be unhurried.
Fact: 25 Fields Medalists signed a declaration dated September 11. Meaning: extraordinary authority and coordination, but not proof that every policy conclusion is right.
Fact: OpenAI publicly claims an internal system produced a Navier-Stokes proof and Lean formalization. Status: the announcement is real; mathematical acceptance and prize recognition require independent scrutiny.
Claim: fast proof generation will destroy fertile mathematical ground. Unknown: it may instead shift prestige toward explanation, question formation and synthesis. The outcome depends on release norms and incentives.
Contested claim: OpenAI improperly benefited from private researchers' work. Status: researchers made detailed allegations and OpenAI denied misuse; do not treat either account as settled.
Falsifiable test: compare fields exposed to heavy AI problem-solving with matched fields on student entry, new concept formation, time-to-exposition, citation/credit disputes, and follow-on human research. More answers with worse assimilation would support the declaration.

Both sides, steelmanned and strawmanned

Steelman the declaration

Research is a living social system, not a scoreboard. Dumping answers without attribution, exposition or time to absorb them can consume non-renewable training problems and leave humans unable to extend what machines prove.

Steelman the acceleration

Hard problems are not sacred reserves. Correct solutions can expose new methods, remove prestige bottlenecks and benefit science. Suppressing capability to preserve institutions would favor incumbents and is unlikely to work globally.

Strawman the declaration

“Fields Medalists want machines banned because they fear looking stupid.” The text welcomes AI that enhances study and understanding; its objection is to incentives and process.

Strawman the acceleration

“A formal proof means the machine understands and humanity automatically benefits.” Verification is not exposition, attribution, pedagogy or a new generation's ability to ask better questions.
