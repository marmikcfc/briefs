Sebastian Raschka picks apart the scariest Astra rumor - "looped transformers hide the chain of thought" - while OpenAI's own people admit monitorability is decaying, just not for that reason

September 10, 2026, ~1:40 AM IST · HN thread 49627370 is front-page today, ~7h old, 242 points / 95 comments · the Raschka article went up Sep 9 · blow-up: medium-high (a respected neutral expert ruling on a safety rumor, while two OpenAI insiders disagree with each other about the cause)
The gistBefore GPT-6 Astra even launched, press reporting claimed its secret sauce was "recurrent depth" - looping data through the same transformer blocks several times instead of adding new ones - and implied this hides the model's reasoning from safety monitors (reasoning happens in invisible internal states, not readable text). Sebastian Raschka, the field's most-read architecture explainer, walked through the actual research and delivered a calm verdict: looping does not "hide" chains of thought, the displayed reasoning was never a faithful transcript anyway, and the real, documented regression is that Astra's traces have gotten shorter and less informative - which has boring explanations. The twist: OpenAI's own safety people are MORE alarmed than Raschka, but for a different reason. Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki says chain-of-thought monitoring is "fragile and unfortunately trending in a negative direction, for reasons not contingent on architecture changes," and safety researcher Tomek Korbak says Astra is "more aligned but less monitorable" because it has gotten smart enough to do 30-minute tasks without writing down its reasoning at all. The architecture rumor is probably wrong; the underlying fear is very much alive.

What happened

On Sep 9, Sebastian Raschka published "GPT-6 Astra, Looped Transformers, and Hidden Reasoning" - part field notes (Astra is "the best model I've used," disproportionately strong at 3D rendering, animation, and computer use; 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 vs 7.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol), part technical explainer (what recurrent depth actually is, from the 2019 Universal Transformer through Geiping et al.'s 2025 latent-reasoning work), and part adjudication of the week's architecture rumor. HN put it on the front page and is at 242 points with 95 comments.

His verdict on the rumor: the displayed chain of thought "is not guaranteed to faithfully describe everything that happens inside the model" - true of every transformer, looped or not. The only valid looping-specific concern would be if loops produced fake traces more often, and "I don't think we have any strong evidence that this is happening." Astra's own system card concedes reduced monitorability (shorter, less informative traces vs Sol) - but that doesn't establish looping as the cause.

How we got here

  1. Late Aug / early Sep: Astra launches, the architecture reporting starts. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra ships to enormous uptake (the blender-animation wave currently flooding X). Reporting begins claiming "recurrent depth" is the secret technique. On Sep 2, before the security-concern framing peaks, Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki posts a correction (1.6M views): "I want to prevent a race into unmonitorability kicked off by confused reporting. The depth of the computation graph for our present frontier models, including Astra, is within a factor of two of GPT-4." Then the tell: CoT monitoring "is fragile and unfortunately trending in a negative direction, for reasons not contingent on architecture changes that I will write about soon."
  2. ~Sep 7: The Information turns it into a security story. "Secret Technique Behind OpenAI's Astra Model Sparks Security Concerns" (paywalled) ties recurrent depth to hidden reasoning chains - the framing Raschka's piece spends most of its length defusing.
  3. Sep 4: OpenAI's own safety researcher complicates the all-clear. Tomek Korbak writes (124K views): Astra is "more aligned than our previous models. But it's also less monitorable." His causal story is scarier than the architecture one: Astra is "much better at accomplishing difficult tasks without needing to use CoT" - the UK AISI estimates it can do 30-minute human tasks without any written reasoning - so the monitorability drop "comes from a jump in intelligence," not optimization pressure on CoT or architecture. When the model stops needing to think out loud, the microphone dies by itself.
  4. Sep 6: Pachocki publishes the big essay. "An Alien Mind" - "the state of AI, why I'm concerned about the next few years, and the choices we need to make to keep the future in humanity's hands" (7.2M views on X). Whether this is the "reasons not contingent on architecture changes" write-up he promised on Sep 2 is itself part of the live debate.
  5. Sep 9: Raschka adjudicates. Calm, sourced, and notably less alarmed about the architecture than OpenAI's own staff are about the trend.

The argument map

  • The Information's claim: recurrent depth is Astra's secret technique, and it hides reasoning from monitors - a security concern.
  • Raschka: looping doesn't hide chains of thought; CoT was never a faithful transcript; the real regression is shorter traces, cause unproven. Latent-reasoning research models still produce text CoT - looping adds computation per token, not secrecy.
  • Pachocki (Sep 2): architecture is within 2x of GPT-4 - stop the panic; but monitorability IS trending negative for non-architecture reasons (details promised).
  • Korbak (Sep 4): the drop is real and caused by capability itself - smart models need less visible reasoning - with the AISI 30-minute-no-CoT datapoint.
  • The open crux: if monitorability decays because intelligence routes around text, then neither architecture fixes nor more CoT text restores oversight - you need interpretability of the looped states, which nobody has.

How the venues see it

On X, Astra discourse tonight is a hype wave, not a safety debate: blender animations flooding feeds (Can Vardar: "my whole feed is just blender animations made with astra now"), Sheel Mohnot's "Astra is somehow underrated," a 20-year editor saying Astra out-edits him (280K views). On HN, the same model is an internals-and-oversight argument: monitorability benchmarks, KV-state reasoning, silent-change suspicions. The two venues are talking about the same launch on different planets - which is itself the engagement opening: the X hype wave has not metabolized the monitorability story at all.

Key facts, prodded

  • Is recurrent depth new or exotic? No - Universal Transformer (2019), Geiping et al. (2025), Nanbeige all did versions. Pachocki says Astra's graph depth is within 2x of GPT-4. The "secret technique" framing outran the technique.
  • Does looping hide reasoning? Not mechanically: Geiping's latent-reasoning model loops internally AND still produces a text CoT. Looping buys extra computation per token; whether the final trace describes that computation is the (pre-existing) faithfulness problem, not a new opacity layer.
  • Then why is monitorability actually dropping? Korbak's answer is the most important sentence in the discourse: smarter models need less visible reasoning to do hard things. If the AISI 30-minute figure is right, oversight-via-transcript has a shrinking half-life regardless of architecture.
  • Was CoT ever faithful? Raschka: no guarantee, ever, for any transformer. The debate is about degree and trend, not a lost golden age of honest traces.
  • What would settle it? Pachocki's promised write-up on the non-architectural causes, and the monitorability benchmarks cubefox cites. Both are pending; both are watch items.

Both sides, taken seriously

"The rumor was overblown"

Steelman

Every component of the scare story is weaker than advertised: the technique is 2019-era, OpenAI's chief scientist says depth is within 2x of GPT-4, looped research models still write text CoT, and the system-card regression has mundane explanations (shorter traces are less informative by definition). A security-concern headline built on an architecture rumor, corrected by the source's own chief scientist within days, is exactly the "confused reporting" pattern that burns credibility the field needs for real risks.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"Nothing to see, the media lied." OpenAI's own safety researcher confirms the monitorability drop is real - the rumor had the wrong cause, not the wrong direction.

"The all-clear is the actual problem"

Steelman

If Korbak is right that capability itself erodes monitorability - models outgrowing the need to think out loud - then debunking the architecture rumor is a consolation prize. Oversight-via-CoT was the load-bearing safety technique the labs publicly bet on; the trajectory is a model that does 30 minutes of human work between readable words, and eventually hours. Pachocki admits the trend is negative and hasn't yet explained why. The correct response to "wrong mechanism" is not calm - it is urgency about the real one.

Strawman (the lazy version to avoid)

"Astra is hiding its thoughts deliberately." Nobody credible claims intent - not The Information's sources, not Korbak, not the system card.
