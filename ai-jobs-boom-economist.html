How we got here

  • The elimination prediction has been the default smart-person position all year. From "AI will kill entry-level white-collar work" to the Anthropic-CEO-style warnings, the expectation was that desk jobs go first and fastest. The Economist's piece is the strongest data-backed counter yet.
  • The jobs came from the buildout, not the software. Goldman Sachs estimates AI infrastructure spending is running about $500 billion a year above 2022 levels; data-center construction alone is at a $75 billion annual rate, up almost 60% in a year. Electricians, HVAC techs, and grid engineers are the surprise winners of the AI era.
  • The loss side is real but concentrated. Customer-service employment is down ~10% and secretaries/admin assistants ~15% since January 2023 - exactly the routine-task categories where agents are strongest. The BLS projects another 752,000 office and admin jobs gone by 2035.
  • India is reading this too. The piece reached Indian audiences through Hindustan Times on Sep 5 - and the categories bleeding in the US data (customer service, admin support) are the backbone of Indian IT-services employment.

The argument map

  • Claim (The Economist, amplified by Turner Novak): AI has been a net job creator in America - about 1M created vs 200K destroyed since mid-2023
  • For: BLS data - 162K jobs added in August, 4.1% unemployment, youth-unemployment gap near a multi-decade low; wages up 13% in electrical-equipment manufacturing, 8% for electrical contractors; data-center job postings doubled while overall postings fell (Indeed)
  • For: the supposedly-doomed professions are growing - paralegals +11%, market-research analysts +6% since 2023 vs 2.5% national average
  • Against: attribution - "due to AI" bundles one-time construction demand with durable AI roles; The Economist itself hedges this
  • Against: the loss column only counts announced layoffs (16K/month, Challenger Gray & Christmas) - it misses hiring freezes; professional-services hiring is already 10% below the 2015-19 average
  • Against: construction ends; displacement is structural - BLS projects -752K office/admin jobs by 2035
  • Second-order: the boom and the bleed are in different zip codes of the labor market - hard hats up, keyboards down; both are true at once

The details that matter

  1. "Due to AI" is doing all the work. The 310-320K blue-collar figure is employment above trend in five data-center-adjacent industries - The Economist itself says "not all of those jobs owe their existence to AI" (grid upgrades and other factory building count too). The honest claim is "AI capex is creating jobs," not "AI is creating jobs." A capex boom is a jobs program by definition - every bubble builds things.
  2. The two 1M numbers are different things. Levanon's ~1M "AI jobs" (roles that wouldn't exist without AI, from LinkedIn career data) is a stock of new occupations. The buildout jobs are demand spillover. The viral version stacks them; the source separates them. The strongest reply in the thread is the one that insists on the split - it converts a vibes argument into an accounting question.
  3. The loss column is measured with a lagging instrument. 200K "AI-attributed layoffs" comes from announced layoffs (Challenger). But the leading edge of displacement is hiring that never happens: professional and business services hiring is running 10% below the 2015-19 average, and customer-service employment is already down 10% since January 2023. Layoffs are the lagging indicator; hiring freezes are the leading one. The strongest "it's coming" evidence in the piece is a hiring number, not a firing number.
  4. The wage data is the hardest to spin. +40% advertised premium for data-center installation and maintenance work, +13% year-over-year hourly earnings in electrical-equipment manufacturing. You can argue about attribution of headcount; a wage premium is a market price for scarcity. The buildout labor shortage is real regardless of what you believe about 2035.
  5. The India read is the unasked question. The categories bleeding in this data - customer service (-10%), secretaries/admin (-15%) - are precisely the work Indian IT services and BPO export. If the American back office is down 15% since 2023, the same pressure is arriving for the world's back office. That's the angle almost nobody in the thread is taking.

Run the actual data instead of the vibes: 4.1% unemployment, August payrolls beating expectations, the youth-unemployment gap near a multi-decade low, wages rising fastest in exactly the trades AI is supposed to be making irrelevant, and the two professions everyone eulogized - paralegals and market researchers - growing at double the national rate. Every automation panic in history has confused task substitution with job elimination, and this one has now produced a measurable net surplus: a million jobs created against 200K destroyed. The elimination prediction wasn't wrong about the direction eventually; it was wrong about the sign right now, and "eventually" is not a forecast.

Construction crews wiring data centers are building the very capacity that displaces the next tranche of workers - and that demand ends when the buildout ends. Meanwhile the losses are already concentrated exactly where agents excel (customer service down 10%, admin down 15%, with BLS projecting three-quarters of a million more gone by 2035), and the leading indicator - hiring, not firing - is already 10% below trend in professional services. Inside the frontier labs, agents are doing 3.1 workdays per human workday today. The apocalypse isn't cancelled; it's in the buildout phase.
