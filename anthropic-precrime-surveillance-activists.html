The American Prospect: Anthropic is building a "predictive" surveillance system to monitor activists - landing on the worst possible day for Anthropic's trust narrative

September 9, 2026, ~10:40 PM IST · HN thread posted 9:31 PM IST (~1h old): 169 points / 68 comments and climbing · Anthropic has not responded to the Prospect · blow-up: high (lands mid-cascade: same day as the Coxon resignation, Hubinger's 10% statement, and the FT's AISI-withholding scoop)
The gistThe American Prospect (Daniel Boguslaw) reports that Anthropic is building an extensive monitoring apparatus aimed at anti-AI activism: a contracted risk-detection platform (Samdesk) giving executives ~60 minutes of advance warning on protest movements, a "person-of-interest" tracking process with routine reports to police, a stated internal goal of "proactive and predictive and preventative threat engagement" (the article's "pre-crime" framing), and a new Global Safety, Intelligence, and Security team hiring an enterprise intelligence specialist ($180-230K) to "track... activism" among other threats. The piece lands on the same day Anthropic's trust narrative took three other hits (a public resignation, an alignment lead's >10% extinction-risk statement, and an FT scoop on withholding its model from UK safety testing). The comment fight is whether this is Palantir-style pre-crime of dissent - or ordinary corporate executive protection wearing scary language.

What happened

The Prospect's story (Sep 9) assembles: (1) a podcast interview with Anthropic's Global Security Operations Center manager Keon Ellison and security operations manager Zach Melvin, describing how Samdesk intelligence gave an Anthropic executive ~60 minutes of warning that a protest had moved up, letting them reroute to a hotel service entrance - "What could have been a high-stress situation was really mitigated through early detection"; (2) a job posting for Anthropic's GSIS (Global Safety, Intelligence, and Security) team seeking an enterprise intelligence specialist to "identify, assess, track, and investigate global threats including geopolitical instability, terrorism, crime, activism, nation-state targeting of the AI sector"; (3) the security program manager's stated goal: "transforming operations from reactive information to gathering proactive and predictive and preventative threat engagement and management."

Anthropic declined to comment. The HN thread is an hour old and already polarized.

Americans for Responsible Innovation is urging the Trump administration to designate AI as critical infrastructure - same footing as water and electricity - which the Prospect reads as the labs' path to federal intelligence sharing and to branding anti-AI civic opposition as infrastructure threats.

Both sides

"This is pre-crime aimed at dissent"

Steelman

Follow Anthropic's own words: a security program manager states the goal is "predictive and preventative threat engagement"; the job posting lists "activism" alongside terrorism as a tracked threat; the company runs a person-of-interest process and reports people to police before any crime; and it is lobbying-adjacent to a critical-infrastructure designation that would fold AI labs into the national-security perimeter where dissent becomes an infrastructure threat. The lab that refused the government mass-surveillance work is building the same logic in-house, aimed at the people protesting it. The word "pre-crime" is fair when the stated goal is prevention and the referrals precede crimes.

Strawman

"Anthropic is surveilling activists" - based on one podcast anecdote about routing an executive around a protest and a job posting. By this standard every Fortune 500 GSOC is a Stasi.

"This is ordinary executive protection, badly framed"

Steelman

Every concrete example in the piece is physical executive protection: advance notice of a protest moved up an hour, an alternate route into a hotel, a referral of a user who named the CEO and a rifle. Tracking credible threats against named executives and hard assets is the legally expected duty of care for any company whose CEO gets death threats; the AR-15 referral is arguably exactly what the duty to warn requires. Nothing in the evidence shows mass surveillance, private-chat reading about activism, or protester dossiers. "Predictive threat engagement" is how every corporate security team talks about not being surprised.

Strawman

"Just standard corporate security" - said about a company that brands itself as the ethical alternative, refused to share the evidence behind its own police referral, and lists "activism" - a constitutionally protected activity - as a threat category in a job posting. The branding is the story; you don't get to be the good-guys lab and the shrugging GSOC at once.
